## LinkedIn feed post (Thu 5 Nov 08:15, attach coc-jlr-linkedin-4x5.mp4)

Good morning, and welcome to everyone who has started following the Chain of Custody case studies this week.

In September 2025, Jaguar Land Rover announced a cyber incident and said it had proactively shut down its systems; vehicle production stopped that month. JLR runs the largest supply chain in the UK automotive sector, and the government says much of it is made up of small and medium-sized businesses employing around 120,000 people.

Whilst its systems were down, JLR set up a supplier help desk and a manual payment system for outstanding invoices. On 28 September, the government announced a loan guarantee to unlock up to £1.5bn of private finance for JLR, and on 7 October JLR announced a financing scheme that paid qualifying suppliers up to 120 days earlier than its typical 60-day terms. Manufacturing restarted on a phased basis from 8 October.

The video sets out what JLR and the government published, followed by three checks I would add to any supplier review: how many weeks of cash a critical supplier holds, how you would pay them if your own systems failed, and which smaller suppliers depend on a single customer.

Sources: JLR statements of 2 September, 7 October and 14 November 2025; GOV.UK, 28 September 2025.
