# Case study 06: Aksoy / counterfeit Cisco — fact sheet

Source (only): US Attorney's Office, District of New Jersey, press release 24-160, 2 May 2024,
"CEO of Dozens of Companies Sentenced to 78 Months in Prison for Massive Scheme to Traffic in Fraudulent and Counterfeit Cisco Networking Equipment"
https://www.justice.gov/usao-nj/pr/ceo-dozens-companies-sentenced-78-months-prison-massive-scheme-traffic-fraudulent-and

OPSEC: the release's military users, platforms and military agency quotes are EXCLUDED. Customers named for use: "hospitals, schools, and government agencies".

| # | Fact (source wording) | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Onur Aksoy, aka "Ron Aksoy" and "Dave Durden," 40, of Miami, Florida | yes |
| 2 | pleaded guilty on June 5, 2023 to conspiring to traffic in counterfeit goods, to commit mail fraud, and to commit wire fraud (Count 1); and mail fraud (Count 4) | yes |
| 3 | sentenced on May 1, 2024 to 78 months in prison; three years of supervised release; fined $40,000 | yes |
| 4 | restitution of $100 million to Cisco under the plea agreement, plus amounts to other victims to be determined | yes |
| 5 | ran at least 19 companies formed in New Jersey and Florida, approximately 15 Amazon storefronts and at least 10 eBay storefronts (the "Pro Network Entities") | yes |
| 6 | imported from suppliers in China and Hong Kong tens of thousands of low-quality, modified computer networking devices with counterfeit Cisco labels, stickers, boxes, documentation, and packaging | yes |
| 7 | made the goods "falsely appear to be new, genuine, and high-quality devices manufactured and authorized by Cisco" | yes |
| 8 | Pro Network Entities generated over $100 million in revenue | yes |
| 9 | devices were typically older, lower-model products, some of which had been sold or discarded, modified to appear to be genuine versions of new, enhanced, more expensive Cisco devices | yes |
| 10 | counterfeiters often added pirated Cisco software and unauthorized, low-quality or unreliable components, including components to circumvent Cisco's software licence and hardware authentication checks | yes |
| 11 | products "would simply fail or otherwise malfunction, causing significant damage to their users' networks and operations – in some cases, costing users tens of thousands of dollars" | yes |
| 12 | customers included hospitals, schools, and government agencies | yes |
| 13 | between 2014 and 2022, CBP seized approximately 180 shipments of counterfeit Cisco devices being shipped to the Pro Network Entities from China and Hong Kong | yes |
| 14 | Aksoy falsely submitted official paperwork to CBP under the alias "Dave Durden" | yes |
| 15 | conspirators broke shipments into smaller parcels shipped on different days; Aksoy used fake delivery addresses in Ohio | yes |
| 16 | after a seizure notice, Aksoy often continued to order counterfeit Cisco products from the same supplier | yes |
| 17 | between 2014 and 2019, Cisco sent seven cease-and-desist letters; Aksoy responded to at least two by causing his attorney to provide Cisco with forged documents | yes |
| 18 | July 2021: search warrant at Aksoy's warehouse; 1,156 counterfeit Cisco devices seized, retail value over $7 million | yes |
| 19 | Criminal Division called it "one of the largest counterfeit trademark cases ever prosecuted in the United States" | yes |
