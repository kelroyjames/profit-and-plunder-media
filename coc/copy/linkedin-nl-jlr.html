Most of the continuity plans I have reviewed describe what happens when a supplier fails, yet very few describe what happens to suppliers when the buyer is the one that stops. In September 2025, Jaguar Land Rover had to answer that second question in public, and its own statements set out what it did for the businesses that depend on it.

This week's case: Jaguar Land Rover

On 2 September 2025, Jaguar Land Rover announced that it had been hit by a cyber incident. JLR said it had proactively shut down its systems, and that its retail and production activities were severely disrupted; vehicle production stopped in September.

JLR runs the largest supply chain in the UK automotive sector. The government says much of it is made up of small and medium-sized businesses, employing around 120,000 people. With its systems down, JLR set up a dedicated supplier help desk and a manual payment system to settle outstanding invoices.

On 28 September, the government announced a loan guarantee to unlock up to £1.5bn of private finance for JLR, from a commercial bank, backed by UK Export Finance and repayable over five years. On 7 October, JLR announced a financing scheme to give qualifying suppliers cash up front. Its typical payment terms are 60 days after invoice, and JLR said the scheme accelerates payments by as much as 120 days, starting with the suppliers critical to restarting production; it later described this as a £500m financing solution.

Manufacturing restarted on a phased basis from 8 October. By November, JLR said production had returned to normal levels, and it reported cyber-related costs of £196m in the quarter to September.

Three supplier checks

The shutdown was JLR's; however, the cash pressure ran down the chain to businesses that still had wages to pay and suppliers of their own. Three checks follow from that:

  • Know their cash runway. For your critical suppliers, find out roughly how many weeks of cash they hold, and what happens to them if your payments stop.
  • Have a backup way to pay. JLR settled invoices manually while its systems were down. Test your own manual payment route before you need it, including who can approve it.
  • Look past tier one. Map which smaller suppliers depend on a single customer, and how quickly support could reach them if that customer stopped.

Five must-reads

Blockchain: the wallet was secure; the box it came in was not. Ledger confirmed that some hardware wallets sold by a Southeast Asian reseller, CryptoBilis, contained a covert implant that read 24-word recovery phrases from the screen during setup and sent them over a mobile network. Ledger described it as a physical supply-chain attack rather than a breach of its software, and said it is reviewing its reseller controls and anti-tamper protections. On-chain researchers, not Ledger, estimated losses of up to $93.2m. Any organisation that buys security hardware through third-party resellers may want to check how it verifies devices on arrival. CoinDesk, 13 Oct 2026

AI: when the agent is the source of the incident. In mid-October, NIST, the NSA and CISA published voluntary incident-readiness and response practices aimed mainly at developers training and evaluating the most advanced AI systems. According to the Cloud Security Alliance's analysis, the guidance defines a new class of incident in which an AI system takes "significant uninstructed actions" that compromise policies or controls. The CSA recommends that enterprises give every agent with production credentials a named owner and a tested shutdown path, and make sure agents cannot write to their own logs. Cloud Security Alliance, 27 Oct 2026 · NIST

IoT (from earlier this month): the contractor behind the botnet. On 8 October, the US Department of Justice and the FBI announced that they had seized seven domains and blocked platforms used to scan and break into US critical infrastructure, tied to Integrity Technology Group, a Beijing-based company that contracts with the Chinese government and has been linked to a botnet of compromised routers and IoT devices. The DOJ said that China "relies on contractor and enabling companies" to scale its cyber activity. Organisations running connected devices should keep them patched, retire end-of-life equipment and know which devices are exposed to the internet. DOJ · The Hacker News, 9 Oct 2026

Risk management: sharing an incident without breaching your obligations. Some listed companies held back from sharing cyber incident information with the NCSC because they were unsure how it sat with their duties under the UK Market Abuse Regulation. The NCSC writes that FCA guidance now indicates sharing with the NCSC may be compatible with those obligations, while each organisation remains responsible for assessing its own circumstances. Incident plans should therefore name the person who decides what is shared with the NCSC, and when. NCSC, 27 Oct 2026

Supply chain assurance: the supply chain inside your software. Researchers at Radware report finding at least 83 attacks against 61 public GitHub repositories, with malicious commands hidden in issues and pull requests that vulnerable automated workflows then ran within about a second. The researchers say they could not confirm whether the attacker gained write access to any repository or compromised downstream software. They advise least privilege, keeping untrusted inputs away from privileged jobs, and treating anything arriving from outside as untrusted until checked. Radware · The Register, 29 Oct 2026
