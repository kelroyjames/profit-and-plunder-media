### This week's case: Carillion

Carillion went into liquidation on 15 January 2018. It had around 43,000 employees, including 19,000 in the UK, and held about 420 contracts with the UK public sector. The Commons committees reported that it collapsed with liabilities of nearly £7bn and £29m in cash.

On 10 July 2017, Carillion announced a £845m reduction in the value of its contracts; by September the figure had risen to £1,045m. The government had rated it amber since February 2016 because of performance concerns. In the months after the July warning, Carillion still announced £1.9bn of new government work, including two HS2 joint venture contracts worth £1.3bn that were approved before the warning and signed after it. The National Audit Office found that none of the contracting authorities believed they had grounds to exclude Carillion.

Further down the chain, the committees found that Carillion enforced standard payment terms of 120 days, and that it owed around £2bn to its 30,000 suppliers, subcontractors and other short-term creditors. The NAO estimated the cost to taxpayers at £148m, and pension liabilities of £2.6bn were to be compensated through the Pension Protection Fund. Work stopped on some construction contracts, including two PFI hospitals. Across central government, contingency planning was complete by 15 January 2018, the day Carillion collapsed.

### Three supplier checks

The rating was amber for almost two years, so the warning was on record. These checks are about what a buyer does with a signal like that:

- **Watch how your supplier pays its own suppliers.** Payment days stretching out can be an early warning sign.
- **Map your dependency.** For each critical service, note which provider holds it, who else could step in, and how long the switch would take.
- **Build the exit plan before you need it, and test it.** A plan written in the week of a collapse comes too late.

**Opening line:** In this edition: the full case video, a supplier dependency map for your next review, and a 15-minute tabletop exercise. These practical extras are not in the LinkedIn edition.

### Bonus 1: a supplier dependency map

Copy these columns into a sheet and fill them in for your ten most critical services. The rows you cannot complete are your action list.

1. **Service:** what stops if this supplier stops?
2. **Provider:** who delivers it today, including any subcontractor you know about.
3. **Concentration:** how many of your other services sit with the same provider?
4. **Alternative:** who else could step in, and have you spoken to them?
5. **Switch time:** how many weeks would it take to move the work?
6. **Signal:** which warning (a rating, payment days, a profit warning) would trigger a review, and who owns it?

### Bonus 2: a 15-minute tabletop exercise

A fictional scenario to run with procurement, finance and operations. Read each step aloud, then give the group five minutes before moving on.

- **Minute 0:** the supplier that runs three of your critical services has just issued a profit warning. Its rating moves to amber today. Who in your organisation hears about it first, and what do they do?
- **Minute 5:** the same supplier is shortlisted for a new contract your team is about to award. Do you have grounds to pause or exclude it, and who decides?
- **Minute 10:** two weeks later, the supplier goes into liquidation. Which service do you restore first, who steps in, and how long will it take?
- **Debrief:** write down the one decision you could not make in the room, and the person who should own it.
