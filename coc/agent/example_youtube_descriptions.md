# Video 1 (v2)

**Title:** 60,000+ Engine Parts, Forged Paperwork: The AOG Technics Fraud

**Description:**
In 2023, an airline contacted Safran to check the authenticity of an engine part supplied by AOG Technics. Safran identified the certificate as a fake. According to the Serious Fraud Office, AOG Technics sold more than 60,000 aircraft engine parts, mostly for the CFM56 engine, accompanied by forged Authorised Release Certificates. The SFO estimates losses of more than £39.3m for airlines and manufacturers. In February 2026, director Jose Alejandro Zamora Yrala was sentenced to four years and eight months after pleading guilty to fraudulent trading.

The fraud ended when someone checked authenticity with the manufacturer. This video covers what the SFO reported, and three receiving checks for goods-in teams.

# Video 2 (v2)

**Title:** Carillion and the Cost of Dependency

**Description:**
On 15 January 2018, Carillion went into liquidation. It held about 420 contracts with the UK public sector, and had liabilities of nearly £7bn and £29m in cash. The government had rated it amber since February 2016, yet in the months after its July 2017 profit warning Carillion announced £1.9bn of new government work. It owed around £2bn to its 30,000 suppliers, subcontractors and other short-term creditors.

There was monitoring, and there were ratings. The problem was dependency. This video covers what the official reports found, and three supplier checks.
